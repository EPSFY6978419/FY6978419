### Introduction: {#introduction}
At a simple level lithium-ion batteries releases or stores charge through a chemical process of moving lithium ions back and forth through a liquid electrolyte, when a lithium atom gets realeased from the anode so does an electron, this electron can't pass through the electrolye so it's forced through a wire to reach the cathode. The lithium ion passes through the electroyle to the cathode terminal and regains that electron see figure 1. The reverse happens when charging. [(Finkelstein et al., 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1039/d4cp00818a)

#### Sodium-Ion Batteries {#sodium-ion-batteries}
Sodium-Ion batteries work on the same back and forth motion as lithium ion batteries, at a fundimatnal level the difference between lithium ion batteries and sodium ion batteries is the different ions, lithium ion batteries use lithium ions whilst sodium ion batteries use sodium ions (refernce). sodium-ion batteries hold on to its capasity even on sub 0℃, whilst traditional lithium ion batteries suffer significant loss in capasity in low sub 0℃ (reference), they are also more stable in harsh temperatures, less likey to expload or get damaged. lithium ion batteries starts to degrade quickly and may expload if exposed to temperatures outside its operating range.
##### Disadvantages:
Sodium atoms are much bigger that lithium atoms (ref), therefore the batteries maximum capasity per size, also know as energy density is reduced, almost as much as 30%, every extra gram adds to launch cost, therefore using this battery can potenially increase the cost.
##### Conclutions:
This battery could be really useful in some applications for satillites, as the extra weight of the battery may potentially add to the cost, if peformace is pioritied then, this battery could be very useful, the extra tollerances to fluctuating temperatures and the abliity to safely drain to 0V means that the satillites could have a higher error margin - creating a reliable satilite.

    <p>
